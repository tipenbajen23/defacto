# Non-digest sweep — findings
NEW p14, p26  CONFIRMED-ERROR. "The controlling test is ... whether that act is coupled with a promise of
   employment to two or more persons." Contrary to People v. Panis, G.R. No. L-58674-77, July 11, 1986:
   "The number of persons dealt with is not an essential ingredient of the act of recruitment and placement
   of workers. Any of the acts mentioned in the basic rule in Article 13(b) will constitute recruitment and
   placement even if only one prospective worker is involved." The two-or-more proviso is a rule of evidence
   creating a presumption. Matters because simple illegal recruitment can have one victim; large scale needs 3+.
NEW p917      CONFIRMED-ERROR. 3rd element of casual employment ("incidental, sporadic or brief") is not in
   Art. 295, 2nd par. (purely residual definition) and contradicts p921 and the UST digest at 922-931.
NEW p2312-16  CONFIRMED-ERROR. 1st requisite ("founded on a company policy, practice or CBA") contrary to
   Home Credit v. Prudente: benefits "voluntarily given ... knowing fully well that the employee is not
   covered by any provision of law or agreement for its payment"; burden on the employee. Contradicts the
   reviewer's own par 2060.
NEW p3090-92  CONFIRMED-ERROR. BFOQ 2nd requisite drops "all or substantially all" (Star Paper, quoted in
   Yrasuegui): "factual basis for believing that all or substantially all persons meeting the qualification
   would be unable to properly perform the duties of the job."

NEW p1358     CONFIRMED-ERROR. "Night worker ... any employed person whose work covers at least seven
   consecutive hours between 10:00 p.m. and 6:00 a.m. (the night work period)." R.A. 10151, Art. 154: the
   chapter applies to those permitted to work at night "during a period of not less than seven (7)
   consecutive hours, including the interval from midnight to five o'clock in the morning"; and a "night
   worker" is one "whose work requires performance of a substantial number of hours of night work which
   exceeds a specified limit ... fixed by the Secretary of Labor." The reviewer imports Art. 86's night
   shift differential window (10 p.m. to 6 a.m.) into Art. 154 and conflates the night PERIOD with the
   definition of a night WORKER. Two different rules; p1360 states Art. 86 correctly.
NEW p794,796  Two further case references with no G.R. number anywhere, not in Revision 3's D5 list:
   "(Wahing case)" at par 794 and "(Cobilla, et al. v. HSY Marketing Ltd.)" at par 796, both cited for the
   equipoise rule. A full sweep of every "X v. Y" in the reviewer found no others beyond D5's list.
